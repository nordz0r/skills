# Interview Patterns

## Choose the Interview Mode

- Use recruiter-screen mode for motivation, timeline, compensation, and basic
  fit.
- Use behavioral mode for ownership, conflict, prioritization, and mistakes.
- Use technical mode for architecture, debugging, tradeoffs, and delivery.
- Use management mode for hiring, delegation, stakeholder alignment, and
  execution through others.

## High-Value Behavioral Prompts

- Tell me about a difficult decision with incomplete data.
- Tell me about a project that went off track and how you recovered it.
- Tell me about a disagreement with a stakeholder or teammate.
- Tell me about a time you improved a process, not just delivered a feature.
- Tell me about a failure and what changed in your behavior afterward.

## STAR and CAR Skeletons

### STAR

- Situation: Give the business or team context.
- Task: State the specific responsibility and constraint.
- Action: Explain what you did and why that path made sense.
- Result: Finish with measurable impact and what changed afterward.

### CAR

- Context: Describe the starting point and stakes.
- Action: Highlight the key decisions and tradeoffs.
- Result: Quantify the outcome and learning.

## Technical Answer Structure

Use this order unless the user wants a polished final answer:

1. Clarify the problem and constraints.
2. State assumptions.
3. Compare two or three reasonable options.
4. Choose one approach and defend the tradeoffs.
5. Mention risks, failure modes, and monitoring.

## Mock Interview Template

### Warm-Up

- Why this role?
- Why this company?
- Walk me through your background in two minutes.

### Core Round

- Ask 5 to 8 role-specific questions.
- Add one follow-up that challenges depth or ownership after each answer.
- Score each answer from 1 to 5 on clarity, specificity, and signal.

### Close

- Ask what the candidate would improve in the same situation today.
- Ask what questions the candidate would ask the interviewer.

## Fast Critique Checklist

- Is the answer specific to the target role?
- Does it include numbers, scope, or concrete constraints?
- Does it show ownership rather than team-level vagueness?
- Does it describe tradeoffs instead of a single obvious path?
- Does it end with a result and learning?
